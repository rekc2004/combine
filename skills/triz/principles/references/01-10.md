# Principles 1–10

| № | Principle | AI-task transformation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Segmentation | Split the task, artifact, or context into independent or replaceable parts; increase decomposition only where coupling hurts. |
| 2 | Taking out | Remove the obstructing part/property from the working set, or extract only the needed part/property; leave the rest in storage or another tool. |
| 3 | Local quality | Give each part its most favorable rules, format, and function instead of imposing one uniform template. |
| 4 | Asymmetry | Replace unnecessary symmetry with differentiated rights, modes, shapes, or checks; if asymmetry exists and helps, increase it deliberately. |
| 5 | Merging | Combine homogeneous or adjacent objects/operations in space or time: one batch, shared pass, or coordinated adjacent stages. |
| 6 | Universality | Let one component perform several functions so other components become unnecessary; remove the redundant components, not just their names. |
| 7 | Nested doll | Place one object inside another or pass it through another's cavity: nested context, recursive module, or genuinely encapsulated subtask—not an arbitrary outline. |
| 8 | Anti-weight | Compensate a harmful load with an opposing contribution from another object or the environment: an index/cache carries retrieval load, provided its maintenance does not recreate the load. |
| 9 | Preliminary anti-action | Before the expected harmful action, apply an opposing action: counterexamples, guards, or failure tests prepared before the risky operation. |
| 10 | Preliminary action | Perform required changes wholly/partly in advance, or pre-position resources so they act without delivery delay: prefetch, templates, prepared criteria. |

Gate: choose only moves that address the stated contradiction; check with A7/A9 rather than applying a transformation because it is fashionable.
