# M4. Eight Laws of System Development

Source: Altshuller, *Creativity as an Exact Science*, 1979, pp. 122–127. Ask one relevant question per pass; these are diagnostic directions, not predictions that every project must follow.

1. **Completeness of parts:** are the working organ, energy/source, transmission, and control minimally operational together? For AI: executor, input/resource, connection, and control/verification.
2. **Energy conductivity:** does required input/signal pass through every part without a blocking gap? For information work, inspect information conductivity explicitly.
3. **Rhythm coordination:** do frequencies, timing, and throughput of interacting parts match, including waits and retries?
4. **Increasing ideality:** which cost, part, or harm can disappear while the useful function remains?
5. **Uneven development:** which lagging part now limits the whole system and creates the contradiction?
6. **Transition to supersystem:** has local improvement exhausted itself so the function must change at orchestration/project level?
7. **Macro-to-micro transition:** can the working action move from whole files/components to smaller events, fragments, tokens, or derived structures?
8. **Increasing su-fieldness:** can a missing interaction become a controllable object–field–object model with better responsiveness and connections?

Gate: turn one "no" into a candidate move and verify with A9. Additional contemporary development laws/trends require a separate source; do not attribute them to this eight-law list.
